## Buy trigger checklist — all three layers, not one (Nov 11, 2026)

A BUY recommendation needs all three layers. Cheap on PEG alone, with momentum still down, is WAIT, not BUY.
1. **Fundamentals:** PEG <1 on GAAP, forward P/E cheap vs history, earnings / guidance still holding, 2-minute story tellable.
2. **Level — buy at the dip level, not after the turn:** the price reached the dip level named in the entry (drawdown tranche for an index, straddle low / put wall / recent low for a stock). Do NOT wait for momentum to turn up or for the price to cross above its 50-day — the level sits below the 50-day by design, so waiting for the turn means buying after the dip is gone (Nov 18, 2026 — user). Momentum (Rising / Falling, MACD) stays in the table as information; it never blocks a dip buy. (The momentum-turn indicators — histogram rising, MACD crossing above zero, ADX >25, relative strength vs S&P turning positive, up-move on rising volume — describe a turn after it happens; they are not a condition for the buy.)
3. **Confirmation:** whales buying calls for a recovery (not puts), filings with no exit (or adding), no corroborated insider warning, no new earnings break / guidance cut.
- One line: cheap at the level + earnings hold + price at the dip level + clean filings = BUY. Cheap on PEG alone, price not at a dip level, momentum down = WAIT.

## STRONG BUY — insider buying + options floor confirm the dip level (Nov 18, 2026 — user: use insider trades + options to make a signal for a good entry)

A plain dip BUY fires on the level + cheap valuation at the level + earnings holding + clean filings. STRONG BUY is the higher-conviction tier above it: the same BUY conditions PLUS both legs below. It alerts (it is a confident BUY) and the verdict cell reads STRONG BUY. One leg only = WATCH, not a signal — the row keeps its standing verdict (BUY, WAIT or HOLD) with the missing leg named in one line. WATCH is the signal status, not a replacement verdict (Nov 18, 2026 — critic: MSFT passed the options leg while its standing verdict is HOLD). For a filer with no Form 4 (a foreign filer such as ASML), the insider leg is n/d, never a pass.

**Insider leg — all must hold:**
- Open-market buys only (Form 4 code P) in the last 90 days. Option exercises, gifts, and 10b5-1 scheduled buys do not count. Opportunistic only — the buyer did not trade the same calendar month in each of the prior 3 years (Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski 2012; see `smart-money-signals`).
- Cluster: 3+ distinct insiders buying within 30 days, OR one officer (CEO / CFO / COO) buy of $500K or more.
- Total buys $500K or more in the last 90 days.
- Buy prices within 15% of the dip level — they bought near the price we would pay, not at the old top.
- No open-market sales by officers in the same 90 days.

**Options leg — all must hold:**
- Put wall (the strike with the most put open interest, nearest expiry with real open interest) at or within 5% below the entry — the floor sits under the level.
- Entry inside the straddle range, not below the straddle low — the market's expected move includes the level.
- Put-wall open interest real: 5,000 contracts or more. A thin chain fails the leg.
- Call-put implied-volatility spread not negative where the data is available (Cremers & Weinbaum 2010 — stocks with expensive calls beat stocks with expensive puts by about 0.5% a week).

**What this is and is not (research, Nov 18, 2026):** no peer-reviewed paper has tested insider buying PLUS options confirmation together — the combination is a conviction filter, not a proven edge. The two legs are proven separately: cluster buys (Lakonishok & Lee 2001; Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski 2012 — opportunistic buys earned about 0.8% a month, value-weighted, routine trades about nothing); signed options flow (Pan & Poteshman 2006 — low put-call ratio stocks beat high-ratio stocks by more than 0.4% the next day and more than 1% the next week). Insiders can be early — enter within days of the Form 4 filing and hold 3–12 months for the insider leg. The insider leg is strongest in small and mid-cap names and weaker in mega-caps (Lakonishok & Lee: the effect concentrates in small firms). Walls are inferred from once-daily open interest, not observed dealer positions, and they move — a wall that breaks can accelerate the fall. Raw options volume alone predicts nothing (Easley, O'Hara & Srinivas 1998 — only signed volume leads). Max pain is unproven — never use it as the floor. The $500K, 15%, and 5% / 5,000-contract lines are [Ours] — no paper sets them; revisit them after a backtest, never from a handful of events (see `smart-money-signals`, Insider-rule backtest).

## Market drop vs single-stock drop (Nov 11, 2026)

- **Market down 15%+ (VOO / QQQ): buy the market, not a stock pick.** A broad -15% is usually multiple compression / fear, not one company's earnings breaking. Recommendation is BUY VOO / QQQ (in tranches: 1/3 now, 1/3 lower, 1/3 only if deeper), not a single stock.
- **One stock down 20–30% while the market is fine: that's a stock-pick moment.** Check why it fell first. If earnings / guidance still hold, it's Lynch-cheap — buy that individual stock. If earnings broke, don't buy it just because it's down.
- Apply this in the Recommendation column: a dropped VOO / QQQ row recommends the index; a dropped single stock row recommends that stock (or DON'T ADD / HOLD if its earnings broke).

If no ticker dropped to a level, say nothing. Never place an order.
