# Stan Weinstein Framework (working distillation)

Use this as the timing checklist. It is a paraphrase of Weinstein's public methodology from *Secrets for Profiting in Bull and Bear Markets* (1988). Weinstein judges **when**, never **what**: this layer reads price, volume and relative strength only. It contains no opinion about a company's earnings, balance sheet or value — that is the fundamentals layer's job (Lynch / Buffett). A stage-2 chart does not make a bad business buyable; a stage-4 chart does not make a good business a sell. It sequences entries and exits on positions the fundamentals layer has already judged.

## The apparatus

- **Weekly charts.** All stage work is done on weekly bars. Daily moves are noise; the stage is a weekly fact.
- **The 30-week moving average is the dividing line** (≈150-day). Price above it with the average rising = advance. Price below it with the average falling = decline. Almost every rule below is a consequence of where price sits against this one line and which way the line is moving.
- **Mansfield relative strength (RS).** The stock's price divided by the index's price, plotted as a line and judged against its own long average (the 52-week average of the ratio acts as the zero line). RS above its average and rising = the stock is beating the market — required for a buy. RS below its average and falling = avoid, whatever the chart alone says. Weinstein never buys a stock with poor relative strength.
- **Volume confirms.** Advances should run on expanding volume, declines and bases on contracting volume. The reverse pattern — heavy volume on down-moves, light volume on rallies — is distribution: institutions leaving.
- **Support and resistance.** Prior highs above the price are overhead supply: buyers from the top who will sell to break even. The cleanest breakouts run into little or no overhead resistance. Prior lows below are support; a stage is only safe while its support holds.

## Layer 0 — the market first ("the forest before the trees")

Never judge a stock before the market it trades in.

- [ ] **Index stage:** run the same stage test on the index (S&P 500). Its stage sets the wind: stage 2 market = tailwind, stage 4 market = headwind.
- [ ] **Breadth / momentum context:** advance–decline behavior and the share of stocks above their own 30-week averages confirm or deny the index's stage. A rising index on narrowing breadth is weaker than it looks.
- [ ] **Rule: no new long buys against a stage-4 market.** Individual breakouts fail at a far higher rate when the index is in stage 4. Cash is a position.
- [ ] **Market-wide precision (the 2026-11-23 misread):** an index below a still-rising 30-week MA is a late-stage-3 market, not stage 4 — that week SPY/QQQ/VOO sat −8% to −14% below MAs that still rose +0.6% to +3.8% over 10 weeks, and the setup was twice misread as "everything is stage 4." The flip to a stage-4 market requires the index MA slope to turn negative **and** price to stay below it for several weekly closes; until both hold, the label is late stage 3 (which blocks new longs just the same — the label, not the action, is what changes).
- [ ] In a stage-2 market, still buy only stage-2 stocks in stage-2 groups (below). The market's stage permits buying; it never selects the stock.

## Layer 1 — the sector / group

- [ ] Run the stage test on the stock's group (sector / industry index) before the stock.
- [ ] **Buy only groups in stage 2** (or breaking into it). Weinstein: buy the strongest stocks in the strongest groups.
- [ ] A stage-2 stock inside a stage-4 group is downgraded: group headwinds cap it and its breakout is suspect. Skip it or demand the full buy checklist with no exceptions.
- [ ] Never bottom-fish a group because it is "down the most." The weakest groups stay weak; that is what stage 4 means.

## The four stages — full criteria

Every stage call states five inputs: **price vs the 30-week MA, MA slope, volume signature, RS behavior, support/resistance behavior.** A call that skips an input is not a Weinstein call.

### Stage 1 — Basing (the bottoming area)

- **Price vs MA:** price has stopped falling and moves sideways, crossing back and forth around the 30-week MA.
- **MA slope:** the MA flattens — it stops declining and runs horizontal.
- **Volume:** dries up, especially on declines. The selling is exhausting itself; down-weeks get quieter.
- **RS:** stops falling; flattens near its lows. Not yet rising — just no longer deteriorating.
- **Support/resistance:** a visible base (trading range) forms. The longer and quieter the base, the more reliable the eventual breakout.
- **Action: watch only.** No buying in stage 1. Build the watchlist here — this is where stage-2 breakouts are born — but capital waits for the breakout. Buying the base is guessing; buying the breakout is the method.

### Stage 2 — Advancing (the buy zone)

- **Price vs MA:** price breaks out of the stage-1 base **above** the 30-week MA and holds above it.
- **MA slope:** the MA turns up and keeps rising under the price.
- **Volume:** the breakout week expands clearly and sharply versus the base's typical weeks; pullbacks after the breakout shrink on lighter volume.
- **RS:** Mansfield RS turns up and crosses above its long average (zero line); the stock is now beating the market.
- **Support/resistance:** breakout clears the top of the base; ideally little overhead resistance above. Pullbacks hold at the rising MA or at the breakout level, which becomes support.
- **Entry (his two-step):** buy half the position on the breakout, half on the first pullback toward the MA / breakout level that holds. Do not chase a single vertical move.
- **Overextension warning:** the further price runs above the 30-week MA, the riskier a fresh buy. A stock far above its MA is a hold for owners, not a new entry — wait for the pullback.
- **Action: this is the only stage where new longs are opened.**

### Stage 3 — Topping (the distribution area)

- **Price vs MA:** price stops advancing and moves sideways at high levels, crossing the MA back and forth — choppy, directionless.
- **MA slope:** the MA flattens after its stage-2 rise.
- **Volume:** heavy volume stops producing progress; down-moves start drawing the bigger volume (distribution). Big down-weeks on expanding volume are the tell.
- **RS:** flattens, then rolls over; the stock stops beating the market before the price visibly breaks.
- **Support/resistance:** rallies fail at the prior highs (resistance holds); the range's support is tested repeatedly and each test weakens it.
- **Action: sell, or tighten stops to just under support / the MA.** Do not wait for stage 4 to confirm what stage 3 is already saying. No new buys — a stock that "looks cheap versus its high" in stage 3 is a top, not a dip.

### Stage 4 — Declining (the falling knife)

- **Price vs MA:** price breaks below the base / support and trades **below a declining 30-week MA**.
- **MA slope:** the MA rolls over and falls. This is the defining condition — no declining MA, no stage-4 call (see the precision rule below).
- **Volume:** breakdowns come on expanding volume; rallies inside the decline are light-volume and short-lived.
- **RS:** RS falls steadily below its average — the stock underperforms the market week after week.
- **Support/resistance:** rallies fail at the declining MA or at broken support, which has become resistance. Each lower high confirms the stage.
- **Action: never buy. Never average down.** A stage-4 stock down 30% can fall another 30% and then another; the percentage fallen is not information. This is the falling-knife rule, and it is the core of the method: **a dip is only a dip inside stage 2. The same percentage fall in stage 4 is a knife.** Holders exit on rallies toward the MA; the method does not hold stage-4 longs hoping for the bottom.

## The buy checklist (all boxes, no exceptions)

- [ ] Market (index) is not in stage 4 — stage 2 or basing preferred.
- [ ] The stock's group is in stage 2, or breaking into it.
- [ ] The stock is breaking out of a stage-1 base, above the 30-week MA.
- [ ] Breakout volume is clearly and sharply expanded versus the base weeks.
- [ ] Mansfield RS is positive (above its long average) and rising.
- [ ] No major overhead resistance sits just above the breakout.
- [ ] Price is not overextended above the 30-week MA (if it is, wait for the pullback entry instead).
- [ ] An initial stop is placed before the order (below).

Any box unchecked = no buy. The checklist's value is that it cannot be argued with after the fact.

## Stop and sell rules

- **Initial stop:** just below the base / breakout support, and below the 30-week MA. Placed as a standing order at entry, not watched "mentally."
- **Trailing in stage 2:** as the advance progresses, raise the stop under each successive pullback low and under the rising MA. The stop ratchets up; it never moves down.
- **The MA rule:** a weekly close back below the 30-week MA ends the trade. Sell — do not wait for the fundamentals to confirm or deny; that is a different layer's question, and the position was a timing position.
- **Stage 3 signs = exit:** flattening MA, distribution volume, RS rolling over at the highs → sell into strength or tighten the stop to just under the range. Stage 4 is what happens to people who ignored stage 3.
- **Never widen a stop.** A stop that gets moved away from the price is not risk management; it is hope with paperwork.

## Short side (the aggressive variant — completeness, not the default)

Weinstein's book gives the short side a full treatment; it is included here because the framework is incomplete without it. It is the aggressive variant: higher risk, same discipline.

- [ ] Short only stage-4 stocks: price below a **declining** 30-week MA, breaking down from a base / support on expanding volume.
- [ ] RS weak and falling — short the weakest stocks in the weakest groups, the mirror of the long rule.
- [ ] Prefer shorting the failed rally: after the breakdown, the first light-volume rally back toward the MA / broken support is the higher-quality entry.
- [ ] Cover discipline: a weekly close back above the 30-week MA, or the stock entering a stage-1 base (MA flattening, volume drying up), ends the short. Never short a stage-1 base hoping it breaks — that is bottom-fishing in reverse.
- [ ] Stops on shorts are as mandatory as on longs, placed above the MA / resistance.

## The classic don'ts

1. **Don't buy a stock below its 30-week MA.** One line decides most of it.
2. **Don't catch a falling knife.** "Down a lot" is not a buy signal in any stage, and in stage 4 it is the trap itself.
3. **Don't average down.** Adding to a loser below a falling MA compounds the one position the method says to exit.
4. **Don't buy on tips, news or stories.** The chart either confirms or it doesn't; this layer has no other input.
5. **Don't fight the market's stage.** Stage-4 market: no new longs, however good the individual chart looks.
6. **Don't buy poor relative strength.** A stock that cannot beat its index in good weeks will not save you in bad ones.
7. **Don't buy into overhead resistance.** Supply from trapped buyers above caps the move before it starts.
8. **Don't chase an overextended stock.** Far above the MA = someone else's exit liquidity. Wait for the pullback.
9. **Don't hold without a stop.** Every position carries a standing stop from day one.
10. **Don't anticipate the breakout.** Stage 1 is a watchlist, not a portfolio. The breakout is the buy; everything before it is preparation.

## What the method costs (calibration, 2026-11-23)

Mechanical backtests of the stage rules (weekly, exit on the stage-4 break, cash earns nothing) quantify the trade: S&P 500 since 1960 ≈ 5.8%/yr vs ≈ 7.4% buy-and-hold, max drawdown −34% vs −56%; GOOG since IPO ≈ 13%/yr vs ≈ 23%; NVDA ≈ 24%/yr vs ≈ 31%. The rules sit in cash roughly a third of the time. Read plainly: **this is insurance against stage 4, not a return engine.** As a timing veto and knife filter it earns its keep; as a sell-everything rule applied to compounders it is too expensive — the exit is easy, the re-entry (stage 1 base → stage 2 breakout) surrenders much of the recovery. Never present a stage call without this cost attached when the question is whether to sell a fundamentally sound holding outright.

## Operational definitions (how we compute it — identical every run)

Computed by `scripts/stage_check.py`; a hand call must use the same definitions and state every input's value.

- **Data:** weekly bars, Friday closes, split/dividend-adjusted (`yfinance`, `W-FRI` resample).
- **30-week MA:** mean of the last 30 weekly closes.
- **MA slope:** the MA's change over 10 weeks, in %. Within ±0.5% = **flat**; above = rising; below = falling.
- **Volume signature:** average weekly volume of up weeks vs down weeks over the last 12 weeks (week's direction = its close vs the prior week's close).
- **RS:** stock close ÷ index close (index = SPY unless stated). Report the ratio's 26-week change and its position vs its own 52-week average (the Mansfield zero-line proxy): above and rising = positive.
- **Distance from the 52-week high** is reported as context (overextension / damage), never as a stage input.
- **Stage call rules (mechanical):**
  - Price above MA + MA rising → **stage 2**.
  - Price above MA + MA flat → stage 2/3 borderline (stage 3 watch).
  - Price above MA + MA falling → **stage 3** (late top).
  - Price below MA + MA falling → **stage 4**.
  - Price below MA + MA flat → stage 1/3 zone: basing after a decline = **stage 1**, topping after an advance = **stage 3**; the prior trend decides, and the call must say so.
  - **Precision rule — price below MA + MA still rising → "late stage 3 transitioning to 4", never "stage 4."** Stage 4 requires a declining MA. (From the NVDA case, 2026-11-23: calling it stage 4 while the MA still rose was imprecise; the Weinstein *action* — no buy — is the same in late stage 3 and stage 4, but the label must match the inputs.)
- **Every call is dated** and names the week of the last close used.

## Worked example — NVDA, week of Nov 20, 2026

Inputs (weekly data to the Friday Nov 20, 2026 close; computed with `stage_check.py`):

- Close **$125.53**; 30-week MA **$207.83** → price **−39.6%** vs the MA.
- MA slope **+2.9%** over 10 weeks → the MA is still **rising** (it is computed over the old advance and turns slowly).
- Price first closed below the 30-week MA in **Feb 2026**, reclaimed it into the **Oct 6, 2026 high of $243.37**, and broke below it again in the fall decline.
- Volume, last 12 weeks: down weeks average **0.96B** shares vs up weeks **0.58B** → distribution signature.
- RS vs SPY: **−37.2%** over 26 weeks, and the ratio sits ~34% below its own 52-week average → RS negative and falling.
- Distance from the 52-week high: −46.3% (context only — Weinstein draws no conclusion from the percentage).

**Call: late stage 3 transitioning to 4.** Four of five inputs are stage-4-shaped (price far below the MA, distribution volume, collapsing RS, rallies failing under the average), but the MA itself has not rolled over, and the precision rule forbids the stage-4 label until it does.

**Weinstein action: no buy.** Not at −30%, not at −48% — the percentage fallen is not an input. What he waits for, in order: (1) the decline stops and a stage-1 base builds for weeks to months; (2) the 30-week MA flattens; (3) price breaks out of the base above the flattening MA on sharply expanded volume; (4) RS turns up through its long average. Only then is it a dip he buys — by which point the fundamentals layer's verdict (a separate skill) decides whether the name deserves capital at all.

**Contrast — GOOG, same week** (computed identically): close $264.00, 30-week MA $342.73 (price −23.0%), MA slope +0.6% (near-flat, still rising), volume balanced (up weeks 0.10B vs down weeks 0.11B — no distribution signature), RS −25.1% over 26 weeks. Same label — late stage 3 transitioning to 4 — reached with weaker distribution evidence: the chart forbids a Weinstein buy, but it is a quieter top than NVDA's, which is exactly why the inputs are stated one by one instead of compressed into the label.
