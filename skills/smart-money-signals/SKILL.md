# Smart Money Signals: one canonical copy

Purpose: one place that says where every smart-money number comes from, what form it is filed on, how to get its price, and how much it weighs in the final decision. The three skills that use it, stocks-report (daily report), stocks-dip-alert (dip alert), analyst-hedgefund (deep dive), point here and do not duplicate it. Change a rule here once and all three pick it up. If a skill's own section disagrees with this file, this file wins.

## Privacy: this skill lives in a PUBLIC repo

Never put the user's net worth, portfolio total, holding $ amounts, holding %, share counts, or cost basis in this file. No user-specific numbers at all, not even as examples.

## Resource list: where every number comes from

- **Company insiders: SEC Form 4** (Statement of Changes in Beneficial Ownership). Source: SEC EDGAR, sec.gov/edgar, via yfinance `insider_transactions`. Due within 2 business days of the trade. Price: EXACT price per share, Form 4 discloses it. Open-market sales / buys only; grants, gifts and option exercises excluded. **A Form 144 is a planned-sale notice, not a completed sale (Nov 15, 2026, critic):** check whether a Form 4 for the same sale has since filed and cite the completed sale, not the plan. PLTR, Nov 14, 2026: the dip alert cited CTO Shyam Sankar's Form 144 notice (300,000 shares, planned) while the Form 4 filed Nov 13 showed the completed sale: 384,680 shares for $64.9M, average $168.80. **yfinance `insider_transactions` lags EDGAR: it is a weak cross-check only; EDGAR submissions JSON is the source of record for the filing count and dates, and the raw Form 4 XML is the source for % of holdings and plan status (Nov 16, 2026, critic: the rebuilt GOOG chart's yfinance pull stopped at Aug 28 while EDGAR had the Oct 29 sale, 82 sh at $284, filed Oct 30).**
- **Congress (Pelosi and others): PTR** (Periodic Transaction Report, STOCK Act). Congress does NOT file Form 4. Form 4 is for company insiders only; congressmen file PTRs with the House Clerk / Senate Secretary. Sources: Capitol Trades, capitoltrades.com (free, every trade, dedicated Pelosi page), primary. Quiver Quantitative, quiverquant.com/congresstrading (free tier). GetMarketAlerts, getmarketalerts.com/trades/nancy-pelosi/portfolio. Official: House Clerk disclosures, disclosures-clerk.house.gov. Lag: 30–45 days by law. No tracker is real-time. Price: NO exact price, filings give amount RANGES. Price = market close on the trade date (say so). Trackers disagree by method (ranges vs counted-buys). Never quote one tracker's % as the only truth.
- **Hedge funds / institutions: 13F** (Form 13F-HR). Source: SEC EDGAR, sec.gov/edgar (search by CIK / fund name, form 13F-HR). Berkshire CIK 1067983, Bridgewater 1350694, Thiel 1562087. Lag: quarterly, due 45 days after quarter-end. The position is 45–135 days old. Price: NO price, 13F gives positions only. Price = quarter-end close. Always state the position date and the age in days.
- **Options unusual activity.** Source: yfinance options chains; SpotGamma / OptionsBell / SquawkFlow. Pull AFTER the open or after the close, when live quotes exist, pre-market pulls are empty (zero quotes / OI). If empty, say so with the pull time, never invent a level. Walls / max pain / straddle breakevens from the live chain. Unusual activity = large prints vs open interest. Volume is activity, never direction, follow the `whale-options-flow` skill.
- **Prices, 52-week highs, daily candles.** Source: Finnhub (via search), TradingView, exchange; yfinance as a fallback. Always verify the price with its date. Current price only, never a stale close. **The 52-week high/low is the intraday max/min over the 365 calendar days ending on the close date, from daily candles (Nov 15, 2026, critic: the Nov 14 dip alert used a stale high).** Never use an aggregator's published 52-week range as the source. It can include a day outside the window. COIN, Nov 14, 2026: the published range high was $303.96 (13 Nov 2025) but 13 Nov 2025 is 366 days before the Nov 13 close, so the strict-window high is $292.76 (14 Nov 2025); the dip alert's −58.01% came from the stale high, the correct figure is −56.40%. The level call was unaffected, the number was wrong. **The window always ends on the last regular-session close date, even in a mid-session dip alert: the live price is compared against the high from that window; only a new close moves the window (Nov 17, 2026, critic: the Nov 16 dip alert ended the window today and printed COIN −58.08% off a $284.74 high, while the window ending on the Nov 13 close gives $292.76 and −59.22% on the same live price).**
- **Forward P/E, forward EPS, PEG.** Source: stockanalysis.com; GuruFocus, gurufocus.com/term/forward-pe-ratio/<TICKER> (quarterly forward P/E history). GAAP EPS first; if only non-GAAP, say so and name the distortion.
- **Momentum / trend.** Computed internally from daily candles (MACD histogram = momentum, price vs 50-day = trend). Words only (Rising / Falling / Flat + Strong / Weak; Uptrend / Downtrend / Sideways). Never print MACD numbers. **Strong / Weak has one rule (Nov 15, 2026, critic: three runs on Nov 14 used three different rules, so their labels disagreed on the same closes): Strong when the MACD line is at least 5% of the price away from zero (|MACD line| ≥ 0.05 × price); Weak otherwise.** The daily report and the dip alert both use this rule. The Nov 14 WITA report (MACD-line-distance rule, all Weak), the Nov 14 PST report (this 5% rule, NVDA, MRVL, COIN Strong), and the Nov 14 dip alert (histogram-sign rule, 13 of 19 Rising Strong) labelled the same names differently; only the 5% rule is canonical now.
- **Company reports: 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, earnings releases.** Source: SEC EDGAR, sec.gov/edgar, and the company's investor-relations page. Filed numbers only, labelled FILED.
- **News.** Follow the `tradingview-news` skill, name the source and date.
- **Sentiment.** Polymarket: sentiment only, never a buy signal, never bet.

## Price freshness: never judge today's dip on an old price (Nov 13, 2026, user: a price older than 15 days can hide a dip)

- A reference price older than 15 days is never used to judge the current dip, the buy call, or the hold call. The stock can fall a lot in 15 days. An old price hides the dip.
- 13F and PTR prices are always older than 15 days (45-day and 30–45-day filing lags). For those signals use the direction only (bought / added / exited) plus the date. Never the old price vs today's price.
- Only Form 4 prices (filed within 2 business days) and live prices are fresh enough for price comparisons. The INSIDER WARNING price comparison is safe, Form 4 prices are at most 2 business days old.

## Weighting: how each signal counts in the final decision

The final decision (BUY / WAIT / HOLD / TRIM / DON'T ADD) is built from gates, not a score. A gate that fails stops the decision; confirmations strengthen it.

### Gates: all must pass for BUY

1. **Lynch (decides WHAT): veto power.** PEG cheap (<1 on GAAP) + earnings hold + story still true. If the Lynch layer says NO, thesis broken, earnings breaking, PEG > 2 with no growth story, the answer is NO BUY, whatever the chart, the options flow or any smart-money signal says. This is the heaviest weight: a veto, not a vote.
2. **Momentum turn (decides WHEN): timing gate.** Price back above the 50-day + momentum Rising = the turn happened. The MACD line above zero is the confirmation that makes the turn strong. A turn with the line still below zero is in progress, not yet confirmed. A rising histogram with the MACD line still below zero AND price below the 50-day is a rebound inside a downtrend, not a turn (five of six names, Nov 13, 2026: histogram Rising, line below zero, price below the 50-day, momentum Rising, trend Downtrend). Correction (Nov 13, 2026): the first version of this gate required the MACD line above zero for any turn. That overstates the evidence, which only covers the price-below-50-day case; a price turn above the 50-day with the line still below zero is a turn in progress, and the line crossing above zero confirms it. Momentum still Falling + price below the 50-day = no turn yet. Cheap on PEG alone is never a confident BUY. The turn can delay a good buy; it can never rescue a bad business.
3. **Insider warning: flag, not a veto (Nov 16, 2026, decided from the papers, see Definitions).** An INSIDER WARNING alone never blocks a BUY and never triggers TRIM/SELL by itself. The papers find insider sales predict only about 1% lower returns over the next month at best, and nothing at all for large companies (Lakonishok & Lee 2001; Biggerstaff, Cicero & Wintoki 2020). It blocks a BUY, and supports TRIM/SELL, only with corroboration: (a) a congressman exiting, (b) earnings or guidance breaking, or the thesis breaking, (c) the sales are opportunistic, not routine, the seller does not trade the same calendar month every year and the sale is not under a 10b5-1 plan (Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski 2012), or (d) the biggest sellers sold more than 25% of their own holdings (Scott & Xu 2004, the idea is from the paper, the 25% line is our own convention). A congressman exit (gate 4) still vetoes a BUY on its own.
4. **No congress exit: veto.** A tracked congressman exiting a held position blocks BUY. A congressman still holding or adding is confirmation, never a buy reason on its own.

### Confirmations: strengthen a BUY, never make one alone

- **Insiders buying (cluster): strongest confirmation.** Officers / directors buying in a cluster, at prices near or above the current price, with their own money. One insider buy is a signal; a cluster is a strong one. The insider leg of the STRONG BUY rule (see `stocks-dip-alert`) is this idea made testable: 3+ insiders in 30 days or one officer buy of $500K or more, $500K or more total in 90 days, buys within 15% of the level, no officer sales in the same window.
- **Congress adding / holding: medium confirmation.** A tracked congressman adding or still holding says the stock still makes sense to hold. Discounted by the 45-day lag and by ranges (no exact price).
- **13F adding: medium confirmation, discounted by age, direction only.** A fund adding in the latest filing. The older the position, the weaker the signal. A 13F add from last quarter is a weak signal; state the age in days. Never compare the quarter-end price to today's price. It is stale (see Price freshness).
- **Options flow: timing confirmation only.** Whales buying calls for a recovery (not puts) confirms the entry; put walls set the floor. It never makes a BUY by itself and never vetoes one. The options leg of the STRONG BUY rule (see `stocks-dip-alert`) is this idea made testable: put wall at or within 5% below the entry, entry inside the straddle range, put-wall open interest 5,000 contracts or more.

### TRIM / SELL triggers: any one fires it

- Thesis broke / earnings break (the "what would change my mind" trigger fired).
- Corroborated INSIDER WARNING: a warning plus a congressman exit, an earnings or guidance break, a broken thesis, opportunistic (non-routine, non-plan) sales, or the biggest sellers selling more than 25% of their own holdings (see gate 3). A warning alone never fires this trigger.
- Two or more smart-money sources exiting in the latest filing, or one source exiting while the stock falls.
- PEG > 2 while the price is still near its high.
- Position too big (>10% of the portfolio, or correlated names >15%).

### The final decision: the recipe

- **BUY** = Lynch YES + momentum turned + no corroborated insider warning + no congress exit + (at least one confirmation, or your own research confirms it, smart money is a signal, not a gate). A warning without corroboration does not block a BUY.
- **WAIT** = Lynch YES but momentum not turned, or a smart-money warning present.
- **HOLD** = Lynch YES, no sell trigger, momentum not turned, or a buy you already own with no reason to exit.
- **TRIM / SELL** = any TRIM / SELL trigger above, with the reason, the size and the level.
- **DON'T ADD** = expensive (PEG > 2) or earnings breaking, regardless of trend.
- **One verdict only (Nov 14, 2026):** the recipe above IS the verdict. Never print BUY MOMENT or HOLD AT ALL lines in any report that uses this skill.

## Plain English, no idioms (Nov 14, 2026, user: "I don't understand this language")

Write every line in plain English. No idioms, no slang, no figures of speech. Never write "survived by a hair", "turn is back on", "flips", "by a hair", "knife's edge", or anything like them. Say it with numbers: the price, the 50-day average, the gap in $ and %, and what changes the decision. If a sentence needs an idiom to make sense, rewrite it without one. Never print a Lynch class name (Stalwart, Fast Grower, Cyclical, Turnaround, Slow Grower, Asset Play) alone. Write what the company sells in plain words instead.

**No unexplained jargon (Nov 14, 2026, user: "multiple compression and leadership discount" is fluff):** never write "multiple compression", "leadership discount", "de-rating", "re-rating", "multiple expansion", or any other finance jargon without explaining it in plain words with numbers. Write it like this: "the price fell 33% from $243.37 to $162.85, while earnings for this quarter are expected to grow about 90% from last year — the company earns more, but the stock costs less" and "the price is lower because the CEO resigned and no successor is named". Every stock's view is plain words with numbers, no jargon, and ends with a clear action line, "Action: ...", what to do, how many shares, at what price.

## Entry price: every WAIT names its price (Nov 14, 2026, user: "wait for what? What price do I have to wait for?")

Never write WAIT without the price you are waiting for. Every WAIT verdict carries one reasoned entry price in $ with its reason in a few words: put wall, straddle low, recent low, and the FPE / PEG at that price. The price comes from the analysis, not from a round number: the put wall with the most open interest, the straddle low breakeven for the nearest expiry, the recent low where buyers stepped in, and the valuation at that price.

## Definitions

- **INSIDER WARNING (Form 4, SEC EDGAR):** total open-market sales ≥ $50M in the last 6 months AND the average sale price ≥ 15% above the current price. They sold near the top while the stock fell. The warning line must read "INSIDER WARNING (Form 4, SEC EDGAR)". Report every open-market transaction with its price per share and the price vs the current price. A sale count without prices is not enough. **Never state the warning without the facts (Nov 14, 2026, user: a bare warning is fluff without facts):** never write "the warning stands" alone. Every time the warning is stated, include the facts, how many open-market sales, total $ sold, who sold most of it, the sale price range, how far above the current price in % and $, what % of their own holdings the biggest sellers sold (from the Form 4 post-transaction holdings column, Table I, final column), and how much of the total was sold under a 10b5-1 plan or in a repeated same-calendar-month pattern (routine) versus a one-off discretionary sale (opportunistic). **A warning stated from the yfinance feed alone is provisional until the Form 4 XML is pulled for the biggest sellers: the % of holdings and the 10b5-1 / routine split come from the filing, not the feed. If those facts cannot be pulled in the run, the warning line itself names what is missing (e.g. "% of holdings and plan split not yet pulled from Form 4 XML"): a gaps section at the end of the report does not cover a warning stated earlier without it (Nov 17, 2026, critic: the SW50 screen stated the CRWD warning without the sale price range, the % of holdings, or the plan split).** **The % of holdings shows its source or stays unverified (Nov 20, 2026, critic: the Nov 18 report stated director Mark Stevens sold "6,121,740 sh = ~18.2% of his total holdings" with no source, while the dip alert the same day said the % was "not yet pulled" — and the run's own tally file does not support 18.2%: its post-transaction holdings column shows Stevens's tracked position falling from ~6.9M shares to ~0.97M across the window, which is most of that position, not 18.2%. The 18.2% implies total holdings of ~33.6M shares, a number that appears nowhere in the run's files).** The % is computed from the Form 4 post-transaction holdings column, direct + indirect rows summed, and the warning line names the two numbers and the filing (for example "6,121,740 sh sold ÷ 7.40M held before the window = ~83%, first sale accession 0001199039-26-000005"). If the filings do not let the run sum the rows (holdings rows missing or split across filings), the line says "% of holdings not yet verified, filing to pull" and never states a % as fact. **The % line always shows its arithmetic (Nov 26, 2026, critic: the Nov 25 report printed AMD's Su as "9.4% of her direct position" beside her Nov 16 sale, while the dip alert printed 2.32% — the run's own detail file and the Form 4 XML, accession 0000002488-26-000213, give 70,000 sh sold ÷ 3,020,666 held before the sale = 2.32%, and no derivation in the run's files gives 9.4%; the window total, 305,000 sh, is 10.1%):** write "X sh sold ÷ Y held before = Z% (accession ...)", where X is named as the latest sale or the window total — never an unnamed % — and Y is the holdings before that sale from the post-transaction column. The two runs print the identical % line for the same seller; the tally file wins and the other is corrected before delivery. **One warning-facts source:** the report and the dip alert share the same facts from the latest Form 4 tally file (form4-tally-<date>.json). They never state opposite things about the same fact. If one run states a fact the other says is missing, the run that follows the tally file wins and the other is corrected before delivery.
- **The standing warning is re-tested every run from the full 6-month tally (Nov 15, 2026, critic: the Nov 14 runs got this wrong two ways).** "No new Form 4 in this window" never means "no warning". The warning is a 6-month test, not a today test. Every run recomputes the tally from all open-market sales in the last 6 months (Form 4 "Sale at price" rows only, grants, gifts and option exercises excluded) and states the standing result per ticker. The trap, verified Nov 15, 2026 from raw EDGAR XML: NVDA's 6-month total is $1,378.2M across 10 sales (director Mark Stevens, 5 sales, $1,353.7M, at $209.70–$234.00, weighted average $221.10, +35.8% above the $162.85 close). The $1,370.5M / 9-sale tally omitted CFO Colette Kress's Sep 18 sale (34,918 sh, $7,648,632, accession 0001588670-26-000014). The Nov 14 filings digest printed ~$968M because it counted only Stevens's September sales and missed his two June sales ($186.0M on Jun 18 + $221.1M on Jun 4), and it wrongly counted a March sale (CFO Colette Kress, Mar 20) that falls outside the 6-month window. A tally that only looks back 2–3 months, or that counts a sale outside the window, is wrong. The Nov 14 WITA report then printed "No INSIDER WARNING on any watchlist name". Flatly wrong while the warning stands; the PST run the same day corrected it. Never print "no warning" for a ticker with a standing warning. Why 15% (Nov 16, 2026, decided from the papers): no paper tests a 10% / 15% / 20% sale-price gap, every paper tests forward returns from the trade date, never the distance between the sale price and a later price. The 15% stays as an arbitrary middle value: 10% sits inside normal large-cap price noise and would fire far more often on routine selling; 20% waits until the ~1-month window in which sales predict anything has already passed. The $50M leg is also ours. No paper uses an absolute dollar total; the papers use % of holdings, % of shares, or the net purchase ratio. Why above the current price: sold above today's price means they avoided the fall; sold below it means they sold before the run. Not a warning. Clearance, the warning turns OFF when (a) insiders buy open-market (a cluster buy is the strongest all-clear), or (b) the next earnings report confirms the business, revenue AND EPS beat or meet consensus AND revenue guidance raised or held. A gross-margin guide move alone does not block clearance; a revenue guide cut or an EPS miss is a guidance cut (Nov 18, 2026, critic: NVDA Q3 FY27 revenue $115.8B beat ~$109.1B, EPS $2.68 beat ~$2.47, Q4 revenue guide $130B ±3% raised vs ~$125B, that clears the warning; the margin guide falling to 71–72% from 74.9% does not block it, because the test is the revenue line, not the margin line), the warning's job is "don't buy before the facts are known", and earnings are the facts; or (c) the sales age out of the 6-month window. A fall alone does not clear it. The fall is already in the price, and a stock can fall 30% twice. The warning is not a prediction of further decline; it is a flag, not a veto. It blocks a BUY, and supports TRIM/SELL, only with corroboration (see gate 3).
- **Congress exit:** a tracked congressman sold or closed a position in the latest PTR, or their holding fell to zero in the latest filing. State the trade date, the market close that day, and the filing date.
- **Congress buy price:** PTR gives amount ranges, not exact prices: the price is the market close on the trade date. Say so. Never present a range midpoint as an exact trade price. This price is for context only (whether the position is in profit or underwater), never for the buy-moment call: it is always older than 15 days (see Price freshness).
- **13F price and age:** 13F gives positions, not prices: the price is the quarter-end close. Always state the position date (quarter-end) and the age in days. The quarter-end close is for context only, never compare it to today's price for the buy-moment call: it is always older than 15 days (see Price freshness).
- **Options words: plain definitions (Nov 14, 2026, user: explain straddle / breakeven / call wall every time):** Straddle = the price of a call + a put at the same strike; it shows how far the market expects the price to move by that date. Breakevens = the low and high prices the stock must reach for the straddle buyer to break even. Call wall = the strike with the most open call contracts; it acts as a top limit. Put wall = the strike with the most open put contracts; it acts as a floor. Max pain = the price where the most options expire worthless. Every report that uses these words prints these definitions under the table.

## Filings view: Form 4 timeline + 13F + PTR per stock (Nov 13, 2026, user: show 13F, Form 4 and PTR forms and prices per stock)

Every deep dive ships one Filings tab per analysis, plus the timeline chart as its reading view. Three forms, three price rules. Never mix them:

- **Form 4 (company insiders): the timeline.** Source: SEC EDGAR via yfinance `insider_transactions`, open-market only, last 6 months. Table: date, insider, position, buy/sell, shares, price per share (exact, from the filing), value, price vs latest close %. Chart: one panel per stock, daily close line, sales as red dots sized by value, buys as green dots, dashed line at the latest close, largest sale annotated (insider, price, value). Dots above the dashed line = sold before the fall; below it = sold before the run; on it = sold near today's price. The full table lives in the sheet's Insiders tab; the chart is its reading view, built by `skills/analyst-hedgefund/scripts/build_insider_timeline.py` (matplotlib). The same chart also carries the PTR congress trades (purple up-triangles = buys, orange down-triangles = sells, price = market close on the trade date) and the 13F snapshot line (top-5 funds combined %, position date, top holder). 13F has no trade dates or prices, so it is a snapshot line, never dots.
- **13F (funds): top holders.** Source: SEC EDGAR Form 13F-HR (stockanalysis.com statistics pages as a shortcut). Table per stock: top 5 funds, shares held, % of shares outstanding, position date (quarter-end). Price: NONE, 13F gives positions only. The quarter-end close is context only, never compared to today's price (always older than 15 days, see Price freshness).
- **PTR (congress): trades.** Source: Capitol Trades (primary), Quiver Quantitative, GetMarketAlerts; official: House Clerk disclosures. Table per stock: member, chamber, buy/sell, trade date, amount range as filed, market close on the trade date, filing date. Price: the market close on the trade date. PTR gives amount ranges, never an exact price; never present a range midpoint as an exact trade price. If a stock has no PTR trades, say so. Never invent one. Lag 30–45 days by law. No tracker is real-time.
- **Maintenance (Nov 13, 2026):** refresh the Filings tab after each 13F season, filings are due 45 days after quarter-end (mid-Feb / mid-May / mid-Aug / mid-Nov). A Filings tab older than one season is stale; state the position date on every 13F line. **PTR parser quirks:** `buy_sell` values are mixed case ('buy'/'Buy'/'sell'/'Sell'), always compare case-insensitively; `amount_range` mixes hyphen and en-dash separators and $/K/M formats ('$1,001-$15,000', '$1K–$15K', '$1M–$5M'), split on [-–—] and handle K/M suffixes.

## What the established strategies use (Nov 13, 2026, user: where do numbers like 15% come from?)

Provenance, read this first: only two of these numbers come from peer-reviewed research. The rest are practitioner rules of thumb (round numbers chosen by data vendors and newsletters, not derived from a study). Both are written down here, labelled.

- **Cluster buying: the strongest signal. [Academic]** 3+ distinct insiders buying open-market within 30 days (some use 14 or 90 days), no offsetting sales, 10b5-1 and option exercises excluded. Lakonishok & Lee (2001, Review of Financial Studies 14(1)): firms with heavy insider buying beat heavy-selling firms by 4.8% over the next 12 months, due entirely to purchases, concentrated in small firms; Jagolinzer, Larcker & Taylor (2012, Journal of Finance): cluster purchases preceded ~8.9% 12-month excess returns; ~14.2% in beaten-down stocks (Opportune Analytics backtest 2010–2023, practitioner, not peer-reviewed). **Cluster selling is NOT a trigger (Nov 16, 2026, decided from the papers):** the cluster evidence is for purchases only. Kang, Kim & Wang (2018, working paper) found cluster purchases earned 3.8% vs 2.0% over 21 trading days, but cluster sales were 'much weaker' and only marginally better than non-cluster sales, likely driven by liquidity and vesting dates; Alldredge & Blank (2019, Journal of Financial Research) report clustered purchases at more than 2% the next month, with no sales result. No paper tests 3 sellers in 14 days. At most, log 3+ discretionary sellers on the same or consecutive days as a weak note. Never a veto.
- **Opportunistic vs routine. [Academic: Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski 2012, Journal of Finance]** Routine = traded in the same calendar month for 3 consecutive prior years. Opportunistic = everyone else with ≥1 trade in each of the 3 preceding years. Opportunistic trades carried the predictive power (~0.82%/month value-weighted); routine trades earned ~nothing. Caveat: a 2020–2025 replication (47,458 purchases) did not reproduce the split. The signal may have weakened in recent data.
- **% of holdings. [Academic: Scott & Xu 2004, Financial Analysts Journal 60(3)]** Only large sales that were also a large % of the seller's holdings predicted significantly negative future returns; small sales that were a small % of holdings predicted *positive* returns. The paper gives no cut-off number. The 25% line is [Practitioner] convention (vendor guides treat selling ~30% in one non-plan transaction as a red flag). Apply per-insider, from the Form 4 post-transaction holdings column (Table I, final column), excluding 10b5-1/plan and option-exercise sales. Selling more than 25% of holdings strengthens a warning; selling a small % weakens it. It is a qualifier on the warning, never a standalone trigger.
- **Notable-sale filters. [Practitioner: Altamira Insider Digest, Nov 2026]** A C-suite sale ≥$1M, any sale ≥$10M, or a sale ≥25% of the seller's stake trips the filter. Transaction size >$1M is notable. A newsletter's screening rule, not research.
- **Our 15% price gap. [Ours: decided from the papers, Nov 16, 2026]** No paper tests a sale-price gap at any level, every paper tests forward returns from the trade date. The 15% stays as an arbitrary middle value (10% = inside normal large-cap noise, more false positives; 20% = after the ~1-month window in which sales predict anything). The warning is a flag, not a veto, see Definitions and gate 3. Do not re-tune the number on small data; see 'Insider-rule backtest' below.

## Insider-rule backtest: scope (Nov 16, 2026, decided from the papers)
- Do not use a backtest on 6–12 months of Form 4 data for ~20 stocks to choose the threshold or validate a trigger. The papers use 16–32 years, thousands of firms, and tens or hundreds of thousands of trade-months (Lakonishok & Lee 2001: 96,147 firm-years; Biggerstaff, Cicero & Wintoki 2020: 227,000 isolated + 70,000 sequenced trade-months). Our data is ~20 firm-years and a handful of warning events. A threshold 'winner' on it would be noise. Detecting a ~1% a month effect needs roughly 100 independent events.
- Minimum credible design: 5–10+ years, hundreds of stocks, 100+ warning events, thresholds pre-registered before looking, benchmark-adjusted forward returns (size, book-to-market, momentum) over 1 / 3 / 6 / 12 months from the trade date, out-of-sample test.
- What the small data may be used for: a descriptive audit only: how many warnings fired at 10% / 15% / 20%, how many stocks, how many events differ between thresholds, the share of warned stocks that underperformed afterwards versus unwarned stocks, with wide confidence intervals and a limits box on every output: 'too few events to rank thresholds; do not use to set the threshold.' Never rank 10 vs 15 vs 20 from this data.

## Research references: why we decide like this (Nov 13, 2026, user: every decision must be able to answer "why, based on what?")

Rule: every metric below carries its source, labelled. [Academic] = peer-reviewed study; [Book] = Lynch's own writing; [Practitioner] = a backtest or vendor rule, not a study; [Convention] = a market-wide definition; [Ours] = our calibration. A number with no source is labelled as such. Never present a calibration as research. When Den asks "why do you think so?", answer with the reference on this page.

- **PEG. [Book] Peter Lynch, One Up on Wall Street (1989):** "the P/E ratio of any company that's fairly priced will equal its growth rate", PEG 1 = fair, <1 cheap, <0.5 very cheap, >2 avoid. The ratio is credited to Mario Farina (1969). [Academic, mixed] Peters (1991): lowest-PEG portfolio $1 → $15.36 (1982–89) vs S&P $3.56; Easton (2004): PEG rankings track expected-return rankings; Sun (2001): inverted U. Low- and high-PEG both earned less than the middle. [Practitioner backtest] Motilal Oswal Wealth Creation Study 2018 (India, 4,015 cases 1998–2015): PEG <1 beat the index by 19% over 3 years, <0.5 by 26%, >1.5 lagged, >3 trailed by 10%. But it used growth actually achieved, not forecast. Limits: Damodaran. Forward P/E + growth from this year's EPS double-counts year 1; buybacks inflate EPS growth; PEG says nothing about debt, earnings quality or return on capital.
- **Earnings growth. [Book] Lynch:** earnings drive prices over the long term; the story must be "earnings will grow". [Academic, the warning] Chan, Karceski & Lakonishok (2003, Journal of Finance): "no persistence in long-term earnings growth beyond chance", analyst forecasts are overly optimistic and add little predictive power. Fama & French (2000): profitability mean-reverts ~40% per year. Mauboussin base rates: <1 in 11 companies above $6B revenue sustained 15%/yr for 5 years; no company above $100B sustained mid-teens growth for a decade. Why it matters: a PEG <1 built on extrapolated past growth is the trap, use conservative growth, demand a mechanism (contracts, backlog, a new product), never a streak.
- **Value: cheap on P/E. [Academic] Fama & French (1992, Journal of Finance; 1993, Journal of Financial Economics):** value stocks outperform growth: the three-factor model (market, size, value). Basu (1977): low-P/E stocks outperform. Profitability: Novy-Marx (2013) gross profitability premium; Fama & French (2015) five-factor model adds profitability (RMW) and investment (CMA), the backing for the cash-vs-reported-earnings and quality checks.
- **Momentum / trend: timing gate, not valuation. [Academic] Jegadeesh & Titman (1993, Journal of Finance):** winners over the past 3–12 months keep winning over the next 3–12 months; Carhart (1997) added momentum (UMD) to the factor model; long-run UMD ~7.2%/yr (French data library). Caveat: momentum crashes, worst month April 2009 −34% (Daniel & Moskowitz 2016). The 50-day average is [Practitioner convention] (StockCharts: 50-day = medium-term trend, 200-day = long-term); no paper says "50-day". The academic version is 12-month momentum. Momentum tells you WHEN, never WHAT. Never buy on momentum alone.
- **Options flow: what predicts. [Academic]** Pan & Poteshman (2006, Review of Financial Studies): buyer-initiated put-call volume ratio: stocks with a low ratio beat high-ratio stocks by more than 0.4% the next day and more than 1% the next week; the effect comes from non-public information and is stronger for leveraged contracts. Cremers & Weinbaum (2010, Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis): call-put implied-volatility spread: stocks with expensive calls beat stocks with expensive puts by about 0.5% a week; stronger when option liquidity is high and stock liquidity low; the effect weakened over the sample. An et al. (2014, Journal of Finance): a rise in call implied volatility predicts high future returns, a rise in put implied volatility predicts low, about 1% a month decile spread, up to 6 months. Easley, O'Hara & Srinivas (1998, Journal of Finance): raw options volume does not lead stock prices; signed volume does (buy call / sell put = positive; sell call / buy put = negative). Johnson & So (2012, Journal of Financial Economics): high option-to-stock volume is bearish through short-sale costs: the lowest decile beats the highest by 0.34% a week; later work (Muravyev et al. 2025) questions the after-cost size. **Walls and max pain are not evidence [Ours / Practitioner]:** put/call walls are inferred from once-daily open interest, not observed dealer positions; max pain has no peer-reviewed test, only strike clustering on expiry (Ni et al. 2005). **No combined test [Research, Nov 18, 2026]:** no peer-reviewed paper tests insider buying PLUS options confirmation together. The combination is a conviction filter, not a proven edge.
- **Dip levels. [Convention] −20% = bear market, −10% = correction (Investor.gov / FINRA:** a broad index falling 20%+ over at least two months). Our −15% / −25% levels are [Ours]: −15% sits between correction and bear, −25% is deep-bear territory. Not research-derived.
- **Lynch's six categories. [Book] Lynch, One Up on Wall Street (1989):** Slow Growers, Stalwarts, Fast Growers, Cyclicals, Turnarounds, Asset Plays. The per-class PEG bands in the analyst-hedgefund skill are [Ours]. Lynch never published per-class PEG bands; he said slow growers are bought for yield, cyclicals on low P/E at the bottom of the cycle.
- **Hedging (collar). [Ours]** Not a Lynch metric and not a return signal. It caps the loss and caps the gain; the cost is the option premium. Buy it to sleep, not to earn.
- **Insider signals.** See "What the established strategies use" above. Same provenance labels.
